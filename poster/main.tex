  \begin{block}{Motivation}

    Modern instruction-tuned LLMs default to flat, encyclopedic prose.
    Specialized historical writing has a distinct lexical and structural
    fingerprint: the Summa's \emph{quaestio} form, Augustine's
    autobiographical address, the Catechism's numbered paragraph
    citations.

    \vspace{0.5em}

    \textbf{Question.} Can a small open-weights model (7B parameters) be
    moved into this scholastic register \emph{cheaply}, on a single
    laptop, using only public-domain or fair-use source texts?

    \vspace{0.5em}

    \textbf{Answer.} Yes. With teacher-distilled supervised fine-tuning
    on $\sim$80 (question, scholastic-answer) pairs, a rubric measuring
    register, citation grounding, and argument structure rises from
    \textbf{19/120 to 68/120} (+258\%) on 10 held-out philosophical
    prompts.

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Pipeline}

    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \input{figures/pipeline}
    \end{figure}

    \begin{itemize}
      \item Scrape CCC (vatican.va), Summa, Augustine (newadvent.org)
            with rate-limiting and on-disk cache.
      \item Clean into 342 structured JSONL chunks.
      \item Claude Sonnet 4.6 as \emph{teacher}: per chunk, generate 2--3
            (question, scholastic-answer) pairs with explicit register
            and CCC-citation instructions. 93 pairs, $\approx$\$0.50.
      \item Supervised fine-tune Qwen 2.5\,7B-Instruct under MLX.
    \end{itemize}

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Training Setup}

    \begin{itemize}
      \item Base: \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct}, MLX 8-bit quantization
      \item LoRA on top 16 of 28 transformer layers
      \item AdamW, LR $10^{-5}$, batch size 1, max seq 2048
      \item 200 iterations, 83 train + 10 valid
      \item Hardware: Apple M4 Pro, 48\,GB unified memory
      \item Wall time: $\approx$\,7 minutes
      \item Peak resident memory: 12.7\,GB
      \item Trainable parameters: 2.6M / 7.6B (0.034\,\%)
    \end{itemize}

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Eval prompts (held out)}

    \small
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item What is the nature of evil?
      \item Is faith reasonable?
      \item What is the relationship between grace and free will?
      \item How does the soul relate to the body?
      \item Why does God permit suffering?
      \item Can a human truly know God?
      \item What does it mean to love one's neighbor?
      \item Is there such a thing as natural law?
      \item How do you reconcile divine foreknowledge with free will?
      \item What is the meaning of human life?
    \end{enumerate}
    \normalsize
    None of these appear in the 93 teacher-generated training pairs.

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Qualitative example}

    \footnotesize
    \textbf{Q.} How do you reconcile divine foreknowledge with free will?

    \vspace{0.4em}

    \textbf{BASE (Qwen 2.5 7B-Instruct):}
    \emph{``The reconciliation of divine foreknowledge with free will is
    a classic philosophical and theological problem that has been debated
    for centuries. This issue arises from the apparent tension between
    the idea that a divine being has perfect knowledge of all future
    events\dots''}

    \vspace{0.6em}

    \textbf{SFT (Qwen 2.5 7B + LoRA on 83 scholastic pairs):}
    \emph{``This is perhaps the most ancient and most pressing question
    in all of metaphysics, and it is one that the Angelic Doctor
    addresses with great precision.}

    \emph{\textbf{Objection 1.} It would seem that divine foreknowledge
    destroys free will, for if God knows\dots''}

    \normalsize

  \begin{alertblock}{Takeaway}

    Small open-weights models can be moved into a niche historical
    register cheaply when capacity is sufficient (7B not 1.5B) and
    quantization is gentle (Q8 not Q4). The bottleneck is not the
    optimization recipe but the \emph{teacher signal}: a stronger model
    generating tightly-prompted training pairs.

  \end{alertblock}

  \begin{block}{Phase 2: scaling \& voice rebalancing}

    Phase 2 scales to 377 training pairs ($\sim$4$\times$ Phase 1) with
    per-source register hints (Aquinas form for \emph{Summa}/CCC chunks,
    Augustinian for \emph{Confessions}/\emph{City of God}), 800 SFT
    iterations. Best checkpoint: \textbf{iter 400} (mild overfitting
    after).

    \vspace{0.5em}

    \textbf{Augustinian gap closed:} \texttt{aug} dim 3 $\to$ 7--13. The
    model now switches register per prompt rather than forcing every
    answer into Summa form.

    \vspace{0.5em}

    \textbf{Strict rubric undervalues this} (penalizes Augustinian
    answers on \texttt{scholastic\_register}). We add a
    \texttt{register\_fluency = max(reg, aug)} metric and a
    \emph{balanced total}; under it, Phase 2 reaches
    \textbf{68/90 vs Phase 1's 66/90.}

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Negative result: DPO saturation}

    The Phase 2 DPO chain (chosen = SFT-v2 output, rejected = base
    output) was inert. From iteration 1: val loss \textbf{0.000}, val
    accuracy \textbf{1.000}, chosen/rejected margin
    \textbf{35.4 nats}. The SFT policy already separated chosen from
    rejected with overwhelming margin, so DPO had no gradient signal.

    \vspace{0.5em}

    \emph{Lesson.} Within-model preference data is degenerate: when
    the policy generated chosen and the base generated rejected, the
    DPO objective is satisfied at initialization. Useful DPO signal
    requires preference pairs the policy has not already pre-learned
    to favor or disfavor.

  \end{block}
