\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a small, fully on-device recipe for moving a 7B-parameter
open-weights model into the scholastic-and-Augustinian register grounded
in the Catechism of the Catholic Church. The recipe is plain: scrape
public-domain and fair-use sources; let a stronger model distill
instruction pairs at modest API cost; LoRA SFT on MLX with 8-bit weights
for seven minutes; evaluate against a coarse register-and-grounding
rubric. Phase~1 (83 pairs, 200 iters) achieves a 258\,\% relative rubric
gain (19$\to$68 out of 120) driven primarily by scholastic surface
markers and CCC paragraph citations. Phase~2 scales to 377 pairs and 800
iterations with per-source register hinting, closing the Augustinian
voice gap (3$\to$13) while matching Phase~1's strict total and improving
the balanced register-fluency metric (68/90 vs 66/90); the best
checkpoint is iter~400. A DPO chain applied on top of SFT-v2 contributed
nothing --- a setup pitfall we document --- demonstrating that
within-model preference data is degenerate when the SFT policy already
separates chosen and rejected at extreme margins. We also reported, as
motivating context, the failure of DPO and ORPO on a smaller 1.5B 4-bit
base, which steered the project away from preference-first methods and
toward SFT-first on a larger Q8 base.

\paragraph{Future work.} Several next steps are immediate.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Human evaluation.} A small expert rater study (3 raters
        $\times$ 30 prompts) across scholastic-register quality,
        theological accuracy, and Augustinian-voice authenticity would
        substantially tighten the claims here.
  \item \textbf{GRPO with LM judges.} Replace the regex rubric with an LM
        judge that scores responses on richer dimensions, then use
        GRPO \cite{grpo} to improve on the judge directly.
  \item \textbf{Scaling.} Repeat the recipe with Qwen 2.5\,32B or a
        comparable model under more aggressive quantization to test
        whether the headroom we left on the Augustinian dimension and
        the structural dimension survives.
  \item \textbf{Multi-tradition catechism mixing.} The same pipeline can
        be applied to other doctrinal corpora, Reformed confessional
        catechisms, Orthodox patristics, Talmudic argument patterns --
        to study how a single base model carries multiple historical
        registers.
  \item \textbf{Augustinian-voice data augmentation.} The
        Augustinian-voice gap is a data problem in our current setup.
        Targeted Confessions/City-of-God oversampling plus an
        Augustinian-voice instruction variant in the teacher prompt are
        the obvious next steps.
\end{enumerate}

We release the code, the rubric, the held-out evaluation prompts, four
MLX LoRA adapter variants on Hugging Face, and this preprint. The
project repository, including this paper, lives at the path
\texttt{paper/} in the public scholastic-llm repository.
